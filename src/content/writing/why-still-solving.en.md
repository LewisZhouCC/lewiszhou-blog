“Still Solving” is not a slogan about working harder. It is closer to a way of working.

When we build complex systems, polished architecture, a successful workflow, or a convincing demo can easily look like proof. But these signals only show that one part worked. They do not automatically mean the problem was solved.

## Start with facts

I want every article here to answer four questions: What is the actual problem? Which facts have been confirmed? What choice was made? What result followed?

> Never present a reasonable inference as a fact, or a completed experiment as a delivered product.

That means fewer perfect conclusions and more boundaries, trade-offs, and open questions. Those are the pieces of context the next decision truly needs.

## Preserve the work in progress

This site covers systems architecture, agents, product decisions, and building in practice. The topics may change, but the standard stays the same: make designs traceable, make outcomes verifiable, and keep complexity proportional to the real need.

A problem does not have to be solved in one attempt. If the evidence is growing, the boundaries are becoming clearer, and the system is moving toward a real outcome, the solving is still in progress.
